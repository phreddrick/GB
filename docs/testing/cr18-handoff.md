## 1. Root cause (what the code did before this branch)

Read from the implementation, not only from the review:

1. **State in memory.** `game.State` (actor goroutine) owns `stars`, `planets`, `races`, `ships`, `sdata`, `power`, `blocks`, `powerBlocks`, the schedule and transient per-turn scratch. `loadAll` reads everything from bbolt at startup.
2. **Three ownership rules, no transaction boundary.** Ships: the store was authoritative between turns (`doTurn` reloaded `AllShips` at turn start, CR-11); some commands wrote ships back, many did not. Planets and stars and races: memory was authoritative and written at the flush; but six handlers read planets through `store.GetPlanet`, mutated and wrote that copy, which the next flush overwrote with the stale in-memory copy (CR-17). Commands wrote per record immediately through about 70 `Put*` sites (`saveShip`, `savePlanet`, `saveStar`, direct `PutRace`, `PutPlanet`, `PutShip`), each its own bbolt transaction.
3. **Writes before the turn completed.** During the turn body: `post` → `store.AppendNews` and `pushTelegram` → `store.PushTelegram` (one transaction each, immediately), and `AllocShipNum` (free-list pop) from `spawnVN` and `buildToxWC`. No other direct world write was reachable from `doTurn` (verified by tracing `planet.go`, `ship.go`, `combat.go`, `vn.go`, `turn.go`: zero `saveShip`/`savePlanet`/`saveStar`/`store.Put*` calls).
4. **How a turn committed.** `doTurn` ended with `flushAll`: `PutShipsBatch` (one transaction), then one transaction **per star** (100), **per planet** (627 in the development universe), **per race**, then separate transactions for power, blocks, power blocks and star data (updates only), and finally `runTurn` wrote the schedule in yet another transaction. The 4.4 s movement segment observed on `gb-dev` with zero ships was this: about 730 fsynced transactions.
5. **Where partial writes left an inconsistent universe.** A crash, `kill -9`, node loss or shutdown-budget timeout anywhere inside that sequence left some records at turn N+1 and others at turn N; ships (written first) could be at N+1 with planets at N; the schedule (written last) said the turn had not happened, so a restart would not even know. News and telegrams of a turn that then failed were already on disk. Nothing stamped the records, so the condition was undetectable.
6. **Shutdown and restart versus incomplete turns.** `Stop` waited for a running turn (good) and then ran the same multi-transaction flush; a timeout exited mid-flush; a `HALTED` engine did not flush but memory could already be half advanced by the panicking turn (plan 8.1), and the store could already hold that turn's news and telegrams.
7. **Competing sources of truth.** Yes: ships (store wins at turn start), planets (memory wins at flush, except the six store-read sites), and the schedule written separately from the world it described. This branch removes the *turn-level* divergence (world and schedule now commit together, and a failed turn resets memory to the store); the *command-level* divergence (CR-11, CR-17) is unchanged and documented.
8. **Needed for atomic, durable turn commits:** one transaction for the whole turn output including schedule and effects; no store write between turn start and that commit; buffered effects; reload of memory from the committed store on any failure before or at the commit; a record that says which turn the store is at; startup validation.

## 2. Design implemented (ADR-0009)

- `db.Store.CommitWorld(*WorldCommit)`: one `bbolt.Update` writing all ships, stars, planets, races, star data, power, blocks, power blocks, the buffered news and telegrams, the schedule record and `meta/commit = {schema_version 1, commit_seq, turn, segment, kind, wall_time, engine_version}`. `PutUniverse` writes the first record (`init`, sequence 1) in the initialisation transaction. Test-only fault hook `SetCommitFault(stage)` at `before-write` / `after-write`.
- **Write fence**: `Store.SetWriteFence(bool)`; every ordinary write method (`Put*`, `Delete*`, `PutMeta`/`PutSchedule`/`DeleteMeta`, `PushTelegram`, `ReadAndClearTelegrams`, `AppendNews`, `PurgeNews`, `PutUniverse`) returns `ErrWriteFenced` and increments `FencedWrites()` while raised. Exempt: `AllocShipNum`, `AllocCommodNum` (a consumed number is a gap, never an inconsistency) and `CommitWorld`.
- `game`: `runTurn` sends the start banner, raises the fence (`beginTurnBody`), runs the body under the existing panic guard, lowers the fence, then either commits (`commitWorld(kind)`) and releases the held notices, or (`failTurn`) drops the buffers, reloads world and schedule from the store (`reloadFromStore`, verbatim schedule, no write) and halts with a reason that states whether memory was reloaded. `post`, `pushTelegram`, `notify`, `notifyRace`, `shout` buffer while `effects.active`; an in-turn telegram is routed live-or-stored by `ConnectedPlayers()` at generation time. `doTurn` no longer flushes; `flushAll` is deleted; the shutdown flush is `commitWorld("shutdown")` after the CR-11 ship reload. A test-only `midTurn` hook sits after the planet and ship passes.
- Startup: `validateStoreCommit` (commit turn ahead of schedule → `HALTED` with `CORRUPT_STORE…`, alive, not ready, no write; schedule ahead → warning; no record → accepted, `commit_seq` 0).
- Observability: `gb_commits_total{kind,result}`, `gb_commit_duration_seconds{kind}`, `gb_commit_seq`, `gb_store_fenced_writes_total`; `commit_seq` in status; `commit_seq` in the `turn complete` log line; `db.Store.WorldDigest()` (plan 8.8 logical digest; schedule hashed without `saved_at`); new `PersistenceNote`; reworded `ErrShutdownTimeout`, `ErrHaltedNotFlushed`, `ModeHalted` comment and the server's exit-code comments and logs.

## 6. Remaining risks and open questions

1. **Command-level persistence is unchanged (CR-11, CR-17).** A command that fails or is killed between two of its own per-record writes is still not atomic, and a command that mutates memory without writing is persisted only at the next commit. This is the next Phase 3a slice (staging set, group commit, acknowledged-after-commit); it needs the 71 `Put*` sites and six `GetPlanet` sites reworked and is a larger change than this one.
2. **`HALTED` carries two conditions.** After a failed turn, memory is consistent (reloaded) but the mode is still `HALTED` and the exit is still a restart; the plan's `TURN_FAILED` mode with `RetryTurn`/`SkipTurn` is deferred. The halt reason distinguishes "reloaded" from "untrusted".
3. **Whole-world commits.** Every commit rewrites all records (no dirty tracking). Fine at this size and faster than before; revisit with the staging set.
4. **Telegram liveness window.** An in-turn telegram to a governor online at generation time is delivered after the commit, not stored; a disconnect in between loses it, as before.
5. **Allocator exemption.** `AllocShipNum`/`AllocCommodNum` write during turns by design; a failed turn can leave a gap in the number space. Documented, not an inconsistency.
6. **Start banner.** The "DOING UPDATE" shout is sent before the body (not held), so a failed turn shows a start banner followed by the rollback notice.
7. **Deployment interaction.** The live `gb-dev` store has no commit record; this binary accepts it and writes the first record at the next commit. Rolling back to the old image after that is safe; rolling forward again is accepted with a warning (schedule ahead). The existing deploy runbook's "not claimed: survival of a crash" statement should be updated only after this change is reviewed, merged, published and synced.
8. **Logging nit observed on `gb-dev` (pre-existing):** `turn starting` logs `turn: numUpdates+1` for segments too, while `turn complete` logs `numUpdates`; unchanged here.

## 8. CR-07: recommended implementation plan (assessment only; not implemented)

**What the Go code does today.** `enroll.Enroll` validates the request and builds a `model.Race` (bcrypt password, attributes, `LikesBest` from the home-planet type, `Likes` from sector compatibilities, conditions fixed at 50, one active governor) and `State.Enroll` stores it through the actor with `PutRace` and adds it to memory. It allocates **no home planet, no sector, no ship**; the governor's home/default system and planet are zero; `race.Conditions` are not taken from a planet. `racegen` is the offline attribute designer with its own point costs (`planetCost`, covariance tables) and requires exclusive access to the file. Preview races therefore log in but cannot play; the endpoint stays off by default.

**What the C server does at enrollment (`source/enrol.c`, `enroll.c`).** For player `Numraces()+1` (refused at `MAXPLAYERS`): map the race's preferred sector type to a preferred planet type (`ppref`); search stars, skipping inhabited stars and stars with fewer than two planets, for a planet of that type that is vacant (no `numsectsowned` for any player) with `RTEMP` within ±50, trying up to 100 random stars, then other types, giving up only when every type is exhausted; set `governor[0]` home/default level, system and planet; copy the planet's `conditions` into the race's preferred conditions; translation 100 for self (and player 1, deities), 1 otherwise; tech 0, morale 0, turn 0; choose the primary sector on the home planet (sector-type census over the planet's map); create one **governmental ship** (`OTYPE_GOV`) from `Shipdata`, landed on that sector, crew = max crew, fuel 0, owned by the player; set the sector's owner, race, `popn = number_sexes`, `fert 100`, `eff 10`; planet `info[player].numsectsowned = 1`, `explored = 1`, `popn = number_sexes`, `maxpopn` from `maxsupport`; mark the star explored and inhabited by the player and give it `AP = 5`; write race, sector, planet, star. (`enroll.c` is the `racegen`-driven variant of the same steps.)

**Recommended design for the Go server (satisfies the eight desired behaviours):**

1. **Enrollment runs on the actor as one typed request and commits as one transaction.** Reuse the CR-18 machinery: raise the write fence, build everything in memory (race, home planet mutation, sector, governor ship, star bits), then `CommitWorld(kind: "enroll")` — no partial race on disk, no second writer, compatible with turn commits and the commit record. A failure at any step discards the staged objects and leaves the store at the previous commit (plan 8.4 in miniature, for one operation). This also means enrollment is refused while a turn is running (`TURN_IN_PROGRESS`), as commands are.
2. **Slot and name validation first** (existing): mode `SERVING`, `playerNum <= MaxPlayers`, unique name, attribute ranges. Add the `racegen` point budget check server-side so a browser cannot submit an over-budget race (port `totalCost`/`validate(rigorous)` from `cmd/racegen` into `internal/enroll`; keep `racegen` as the offline designer that now *submits* over HTTP instead of writing the database).
3. **Home-planet allocation = `enrol.c`'s search, seeded from the actor's RNG** (Phase 3b will own the RNG; until then the existing `intRand`): preferred planet type from the race's `LikesBest`; candidate stars not inhabited by anyone and with ≥ 2 planets; candidate planets of the preferred type, vacant (no `Info[i].NumSectsOwned`), `Conditions[RTEMP]` in ±50; fall back through the other planet types; `ErrNoHomePlanet` when none remains (reported to the player, nothing committed).
4. **Starting state exactly as C:** copy the planet's `Conditions` into `race.Conditions` (replaces the fixed 50s); governor 0 home/default level/system/planet; translate table; primary sector chosen by the C census rule on the planet's sector map (the Go `Planet` embeds its sectors); sector owner/race/popn/fert/eff; planet `Info[p]` (`NumSectsOwned 1`, `Explored`, `Popn`), `MaxPopn` via the existing `maxsupport` port; star `Explored`/`Inhabited` bits and `AP[p] = 5`; one `OtypeGov` ship built from the ship table (`build.go` already has the ship-creation helpers and `AllocShipNum`), landed on the sector, `Popn = MaxCrew`, orbit chain inserted (`rebuildOrbitChains` logic or the planet's `Ships` head).
5. **Playable without restart:** the race is appended to `s.races`, the planet/star/ship are already the in-memory objects; `publishWorld` refreshes counters; `Authenticate` sees the race immediately (already the case for preview races).
6. **Interface and controls:** keep `GB_ENROLL_PREVIEW` as the gate name but change its meaning to "enrollment open" once the race is playable, or introduce `GB_ENROLL_OPEN` and retire the preview notice; rate-limit and size-limit the endpoint (CR-20-adjacent); log and count `gb_enrollments_total{result}` (exists). Decision 9 of the plan (enrollment disabled by default) stays until the owner opens it.
7. **Tests:** golden-style comparison of the home-planet search and starting state against the C rules on a seeded fixture universe (the same technique as `test/ctool`, if `enrol.c` can be compiled into the tool; otherwise a table of expected outcomes read from the C source); enrollment-commit atomicity (fault injection: nothing on disk, race absent, slot free); concurrency (two enrollments for the last slot: one succeeds); enrollment refused during a turn; login and a first command (`survey`, `map`) succeed after enrollment without restart; restart preserves the race, ship and sector.
8. **Not in scope for CR-07:** the market, victory and other turn mechanics; the GUI designer; multiple governors at enrollment.

**Order of work after CR-18 review:** (a) agree the enrollment behaviour and the gate name with the owner; (b) port the point-budget validation; (c) implement the home-planet search and starting state behind the fence/commit; (d) tests; (e) `racegen` becomes a client of the endpoint; (f) documentation (`local-server.md` section 6, deploy runbook player-access section). Estimated size: comparable to this branch (a few hundred lines of engine code plus tests), touching `internal/enroll`, `internal/game/admin.go`, `internal/game/build.go` helpers and `cmd/racegen`.
