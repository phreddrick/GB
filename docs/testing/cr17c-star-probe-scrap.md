# CR-17C star-orbit Space Probe scrap (#43)

An explicit numeric `scrap #N` can destroy a living, undocked Space Probe in its star's exterior list. This slice requires zero maximum crew, civilians and troops, no children, and a probe other than the caller's government ship. These conditions exclude recovery, hangar changes, government and morale effects. A valid session and ownership suffice for Go numeric selection, including a probe assigned to another governor. The original C also checks the selected ship's governor (source/scrap.c). The existing Go numeric aliases and ignored trailing arguments are supported. A wide number that aliases a supported ship through legacy integer narrowing is refused.

On success one star AP is spent. The probe receives the caller's number in WhoKilled, with Alive and Notified cleared. Fuel, mass, cargo, destination order, other players' AP, and list links are preserved. The actor installs both records into the existing objects before sending the original no-salvage and Destroyed messages. Zero AP uses the existing registry refusal for the calling governor. A repeat selection of the dead probe finds no living ship and costs nothing. The normal scheduled orbit rebuild later unlinks the dead row. Scrap does not allocate or free its number.

## Admission and recovery

The actor and storage use the same closed planner. The bbolt write transaction compares the complete race, star and ordered exterior ship rows, checks the existing write fence and maintenance guard, and writes the star and the probe. Numeric selection is independent of the directory. A navigation order on an undocked probe and stale incoming links from other ships cause no additional scrap writes. A surviving claimant can explicitly release its own link under the existing star undock rules, including after the probe is detached by a turn. A physically held historical probe that still declares star orbit can be refused when a complete disjoint hold explains its absence.

Legacy exterior construction can save new ships before the star head. After a cold open, a detached list may end at any current exterior member or at zero. A later prepend may have moved the head. Scrap applies its Space Probe eligibility check before the shared read-only list check. Friendly dock applies the list check to either missing selection, regardless of design or crew. They validate identities, star locations and outgoing links, require living, childless ships in the detached portion, and check other rooted orbits and holds for overlapping membership. A legacy type-selector dock can name a detached target by number. Its exterior peer claim must name an existing ship with a valid identity, but it does not add the target to a physical list. The peer can have a different outgoing claim after another dock or release. The selected probe must still be undocked to use atomic scrap. Unrooted incoming links from another build do not themselves establish membership. An agreed legacy state refuses without spending AP, changing ships or emitting success. Valid pending actor/store differences defer. The scheduled turn rebuild can restore membership for a manual retry.

Malformed roots or links, late conflicts, uncertain writes and loss of actor ownership during installation halt the engine with stale shutdown flushing suppressed. The command uses the existing CR-17 transaction and uncertainty handling. Type and wildcard selections, other orbits and ship types, docked ships, government ships and destruction with children or crew remain on the legacy path. General salvage, recursive destruction parity and dead-ship recycling remain separate work.

## Regression coverage

On the base, a real dispatcher observer saw a live stored probe and five AP at Destroyed, then a dead stored probe and five AP after return. The tests compare both durable records and actor objects at both output messages, and compare the whole logical store after the command. They include zero, one and multiple AP, historical holds and incoming claims, authority, selector routing, stale witnesses, uncertainty and shutdown suppression. Subprocess SIGKILLs cover both writes, both sides of commit, and actor installation. Each process case compares the database immediately on reopen. Detached-build regressions dispatch both ordinary and God construction, discard the test actor without flushing, and reopen the saved world. They cover an actor-only build prepend, a persisted star-orbit Carrier launch prepend, both dock roles, and manual dock, undock and scrap after a scheduled segment, full update and restart. Synthetic controls exercise a zero tail and structural contradictions. A further postcommit SIGKILL with a stale claimant carries the same reopened database through a scheduled segment, full update and restart. It checks unlinking, normal update AP income, repeat selection and claimant recovery.
